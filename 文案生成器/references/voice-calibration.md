# Voice Calibration

Voice is the difference between copy that sounds like the brand and copy that sounds like a template. Calibrate on five dials, then write to them.

## The five dials

| Dial | Range | What it controls |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | 4–8 words ← → 16–20 words | Pace. Short = urgent, blunt, athletic. Long = considered, premium, literary |
| Vocabulary | plain ← → technical/literate | "picks up crumbs" vs "agitates particulate" |
| Person | "we/our" ← → "you" ← → impersonal | Products for self-expression use "you"; luxury heritage uses "we"; tools often impersonal |
| Punctuation energy | zero exclamation marks, restrained ← → dashes, fragments, exclamations | Sets the humour ceiling |
| Claim style | bare fact ← → benefit-forward warmth | Does the copy sell a spec or a feeling? |

Typical consumer PDP targets: sentence length 8–14 words, second person, at most one exclamation mark in the whole page, benefit-forward.

## Archetypes

### Quiet Premium — skincare, fragrance, audio, eyewear
- Sentences 10–16 words, no exclamations, low adverb density.
- Opens with a plain statement of effect, not a promise.
- Bullet: **Sinks in clean.**  Weightless gel, fragrance-free, absorbs in under a minute.
- Never: "game-changer", "must-have", "OMG", emoji, all-caps.
- Slot: calm, precise, a little reserved.

### Warm Everyday — cookware, storage, cleaning, home textiles
- Sentences 8–14 words, second person, friendly but not cute.
- Bullet: **Fits a whole chicken.**  5.5 qt, wide enough to brown in one batch.
- Never: luxury abstraction, jargon, scolding.
- Slot: competent, unshowy, on the buyer's side.

### Bright Playful — snacks, gen-Z beauty, stationery, pets
- Sentences 5–11 words, fragments welcome, one exclamation mark maximum and only for real delight.
- Bullet: **Crunch you can hear.**  Kettle-cooked in small batches.
- Never: sarcasm at the buyer's expense, forced slang, borrowed internet idiom.
- Slot: energetic, specific, never trying too hard.

### Plain-Spoken Expert — tools, tech accessories, outdoors, automotive
- Sentences 8–15 words, concrete nouns, zero adjectives that cannot be measured.
- Bullet: **Holds 200 in-lb.**  Hardened CR-V head, no rounding on rusted bolts.
- Never: marketing abstraction, lifestyle fluff.
- Slot: the person behind the counter who has actually used it.

### Clinical Trust — supplements-adjacent, dental, medical devices, baby safety
- Sentences 9–15 words, hedged precisely ("supports", "helps maintain" — never "cures").
- Bullet: **No added sugar.**  Sweetened with monk fruit; 2 g sugar alcohol per bar.
- Never: any outcome claim the source does not substantiate. Read the compliance doc before writing a line.
- Slot: measured, transparent, conservative.

### Design-Led Minimal — furniture, lighting, stationery, accessories
- Sentences 6–12 words, few adjectives, confident whitespace.
- Bullet: **Sits flush.**  0.6 in gap, no bracket visibility.
- Never: exclamation marks, warmth-forced banter, feature lists.
- Slot: still, precise, lets the object speak.

## The single calibrating question

When voice is unknown, ask **exactly one** question. Pick the archetype that fits the category, then confirm with an A/B that is about *sound*, not about adjectives:

> "Which line sounds more like you?
> **A** — *Engineered to disappear on your wrist.*
> **B** — *You'll forget you're even wearing it.*"

Other usable pairs:
- **A** *No rust. No smell. No scrubbing.* / **B** *Keeps its shine, season after season.*
- **A** *Crunchy, salty, gone in a day.* / **B** *Small-batch kettle-cooked, lightly salted.*
- **A** *Holds 200 in-lb without rounding.* / **B** *Built for the bolts nothing else will budge.*

One question. Two options. Then commit — do not stack follow-up questions about voice.

## Derivations from the category

If there is no time to ask, infer from the archetype table by category. Infer, state the assumption in the delivery notes, and move on. Never let a missing voice brief stall the copy.

## Recording the voice

Once settled, fill `assets/voice-card.md`. It is the consistency contract for this product and every future revision — re-reading it beats re-deriving the voice next quarter.

## Consistency checks

- Does the hook, the bullets, and the CTA sound like the same person?
- Is sentence length consistent within a block, even if it varies between blocks?
- Would a reader who only sees the bullets still identify the brand?
- Count the adjectives. More than roughly one per sentence means the copy is decorating instead of informing.
